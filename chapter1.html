CHAPTER 1

COMPUTER SYSTEM

This chapter helps us understand what a computer actually is, how it developed over time, and what its important parts are. Before learning programming or software in the later chapters, it is necessary to understand the machine itself first. This chapter is divided into two main units - Introduction of Computer, and Computer System and I/O Devices.

1.1 Introduction of Computer

1.1.1 Definition, Characteristics and Application of Computer

Definition

A computer is an electronic device that accepts data as input, processes the data according to a set of instructions (a program), and gives the result as output. In simple words, a computer is a data processing machine. It cannot think on its own; it only works based on the instructions given to it by the user or a programmer.

The word "computer" is derived from the word "compute" which means "to calculate". Earlier computers were mainly used for calculation, but modern computers are used for almost every kind of task, from writing documents to controlling spacecraft.

Characteristics of Computer

  1. Speed - A computer can perform millions of calculations in a second. Work that would take a human several days can be completed by a computer within seconds.
  2. Accuracy - Computers give error-free results as long as correct data and instructions are supplied. Most errors that occur are actually human errors, not computer errors.
  3. Storage - A computer can store a huge amount of data and information permanently in its secondary storage devices and can retrieve it whenever required.
  4. Diligence - A computer does not get tired, bored or lose concentration even after doing the same work repeatedly for hours.
  5. Versatility - The same computer can be used to prepare a document, play music, browse the internet, and calculate a budget - it can perform many different types of tasks.
  6. Automation - Once a program is given, the computer can carry out the task automatically without step by step human help.
  7. No I.Q. of its own - A computer cannot take its own decisions; it only follows the instructions given to it.

Applications of Computer

Computers are used almost everywhere in today's world. Some common fields of application are listed below.

  • Education - online classes, digital library, e-learning materials
  • Business - billing, accounting, inventory and payroll management
  • Medical field - diagnosis, scanning (CT, MRI), patient record keeping
  • Communication - email, video calling, social media
  • Banking - ATM, online banking, mobile banking
  • Entertainment - games, movies, music
  • Government sector - record keeping, e-governance, online services
  • Scientific research - simulation, weather forecasting, space research

A computer motherboard - the main circuit board that connects all the parts of a computer

1.1.2 Evolution of Computer Technology

Computers did not become powerful overnight. They have developed through different stages, generally called "generations". Each generation is identified mainly by the type of technology used to build the computer.

Generation Period Technology Used Example
First Generation 1942 - 1955 Vacuum Tubes ENIAC, UNIVAC
Second Generation 1955 - 1964 Transistors IBM 1401
Third Generation 1964 - 1975 Integrated Circuits (IC) IBM 360
Fourth Generation 1975 - 1989 Microprocessor (VLSI) IBM PC, Apple II
Fifth Generation 1989 - Present ULSI and Artificial Intelligence Modern laptops, smartphones

As we can see from the table above, each generation of computers became smaller in size, faster in speed, cheaper in cost, and more reliable than the previous one.

1.1.4 Super, Mainframe, Mini and Microcomputers

Computers can be classified into different categories according to their size, processing power, speed and cost.

Supercomputer
The fastest and most expensive type of computer, used for highly complex calculations such as weather forecasting, nuclear research and space exploration. Example: PARAM, Cray.
Mainframe Computer
A large, powerful and expensive computer used by big organizations like banks and airlines to process bulk data and serve many users (terminals) at the same time. Example: IBM Z-series.
Minicomputer
A mid-sized computer, more powerful than a microcomputer but smaller than a mainframe. Used by medium-sized businesses. Example: PDP-11.
Microcomputer
The smallest and cheapest type of computer, built around a microprocessor, used for personal and everyday tasks. Example: Desktop PC, Laptop.

1.1.5 Mobile Computing and its Application

Mobile computing refers to the use of portable computing devices such as smartphones, tablets and laptops, that allow people to access data and perform computing tasks while moving from one place to another, usually with the help of wireless communication (Wi-Fi, mobile data, Bluetooth).

Applications of Mobile Computing

  • Mobile banking and digital wallets (e.g. eSewa, Khalti)
  • GPS navigation and location tracking
  • Video calling and instant messaging
  • Online shopping and food delivery apps
  • Mobile learning (e-learning apps)
  • Health monitoring apps and fitness trackers

1.2 Computer System and I/O Devices

1.2.1 Concept of Computer Architecture and Organization

Computer architecture refers to the logical and conceptual design of a computer system - it describes what the different parts of a computer are supposed to do, i.e. the specification and behaviour as seen by the programmer (for example, the instruction set, registers, and data types).

Computer organization, on the other hand, refers to how those functions are actually implemented in hardware, i.e. how the circuits, processor and memory are physically arranged and connected to achieve the architecture. In short, architecture is "what it does" and organization is "how it does it".

1.2.2 Components of Computer System

A complete computer system is made up of four basic units that work together.

  1. Input Unit - accepts data and instructions from the user (e.g. keyboard, mouse)
  2. Central Processing Unit (CPU) - processes the data. It has two main parts: Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) and Control Unit (CU)
  3. Memory Unit - stores data, instructions and results temporarily or permanently
  4. Output Unit - displays or presents the processed result to the user (e.g. monitor, printer)

1.2.3 Microprocessor

A microprocessor is a single silicon chip that contains the complete Central Processing Unit (Control Unit + ALU + registers) of a computer. It is often called the "brain" of the computer because all processing and calculations take place inside it.

  • Clock Speed - the number of instruction cycles the processor can carry out per second, measured in GHz
  • Word Length - the number of bits a processor can handle at one time (e.g. 32-bit, 64-bit processors)
  • ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit) - performs arithmetic operations (add, subtract, etc.) and logical operations (comparison, AND, OR, etc.)
  • Control Unit (CU) - directs and coordinates the activities of all other units of the computer, but does not process data itself
  • Registers - very small, high-speed storage locations inside the CPU used to hold data temporarily during processing

1.2.4 Bus System

A bus is a set of parallel wires/lines used to transfer data between different components of the computer.

Type of BusFunction
Data BusCarries the actual data between the CPU, memory and I/O devices. It is bi-directional.
Address BusCarries the memory address that specifies where the data should be sent or read from. It is uni-directional.
Control BusCarries control signals (like read, write, interrupt) that manage and coordinate the activities of the computer.

1.2.5 Primary Memory

Primary memory (also called main memory) is directly accessible by the CPU and is used to store data and instructions that are currently being used by the computer. It is generally faster and smaller in size compared to secondary memory.

RAM (Random Access Memory)

RAM is a volatile memory, which means the data stored in it is lost when the power is turned off. It is used to temporarily store data and programs that the CPU is currently working on.

  • SRAM (Static RAM) - faster and more expensive; does not need to be refreshed; used for cache memory
  • DRAM (Dynamic RAM) - slower and cheaper; needs to be refreshed thousands of times per second; used as main memory

A RAM module - volatile primary memory of the computer

ROM (Read Only Memory)

ROM is a non-volatile memory, meaning the data remains even after the power is switched off. It usually contains permanent instructions such as the BIOS/bootstrap program, which is required to start the computer.

  • PROM (Programmable ROM) - can be programmed once by the user using a special device
  • EPROM (Erasable Programmable ROM) - can be erased using ultraviolet light and reprogrammed
  • EEPROM (Electrically Erasable Programmable ROM) - can be erased and reprogrammed electrically, without removing the chip

Cache Memory

Cache memory is a very small, very fast memory placed between the CPU and main memory. It stores frequently used data and instructions so that the CPU does not have to fetch them from the slower main memory again and again, which increases the overall speed of the system.

Buffer

A buffer is a temporary storage area, usually in RAM, used to hold data while it is being transferred between two devices that work at different speeds (for example, between a computer and a printer). It prevents data loss and smooths out the difference in speed.

1.2.6 Secondary Memory

Secondary memory (also called auxiliary or external storage) is non-volatile memory used to store data and programs permanently. It has a much larger storage capacity than primary memory but is slower to access.

  • Magnetic Disk - e.g. Hard Disk Drive (HDD), which stores data using magnetized spots on rotating circular platters
  • Optical Disk - e.g. CD, DVD, Blu-ray, which stores data using tiny pits and lands read by a laser beam
  • Flash Memory - e.g. Pen drive/USB drive, memory card, Solid State Drive (SSD), which stores data electronically with no moving parts

1.2.7 Input Devices

Input devices are hardware used to enter data and instructions into the computer.

Keyboard
The most common input device, used to type text, numbers and commands.
Mouse
A pointing device used to select, click, drag and move objects on the screen.
Scanner
Converts printed documents, pictures or photographs into a digital image that can be stored and edited on a computer.
Light Pen
A light-sensitive pointing device used to select objects or draw directly on the screen.
OMR (Optical Mark Reader/Recognition)
Detects marks made by pen or pencil on a special form, commonly used to check answer sheets of objective (MCQ) exams.
OCR (Optical Character Reader/Recognition)
Reads printed or typed text and converts it into an editable digital text document.
BCR (Bar Code Reader)
Reads barcodes (parallel lines of varying width) printed on products, commonly used in supermarkets for billing.
MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Reader/Recognition)
Reads characters printed with special magnetic ink, mainly used by banks to process cheques quickly and securely.
Touch Screen
Allows the user to give input by directly touching the display screen with a finger or stylus.
Microphone
Converts sound/voice into an electrical/digital signal that can be processed and stored by the computer.
Digital Camera
Captures photographs and videos in digital form, which can then be transferred to a computer.
  

A bar code reader (BCR), commonly used at shop counters for billing

1.2.8 Output Devices

Output devices are hardware used to present the processed result to the user in a readable/usable form.

Monitor
A visual display unit (VDU) that shows text, images and video output on its screen.
LCD (Liquid Crystal Display)
A flat panel display that uses liquid crystals and a backlight to produce images; thinner and consumes less power than old CRT monitors.
LED (Light Emitting Diode) Display
Similar to LCD but uses LEDs for backlighting instead of a fluorescent lamp, giving better brightness, contrast and lower power consumption.
Dot Matrix Printer
An impact printer that forms characters using a matrix of small pins striking an ink ribbon; noisy and low quality, but cheap and can print multiple carbon copies at once.
Inkjet Printer
A non-impact printer that sprays tiny droplets of liquid ink onto paper to form characters and images; gives good quality colour prints at low cost.
Laser Printer
A non-impact printer that uses a laser beam and toner powder to print; gives fast, high-quality output and is commonly used in offices.
Speaker
An output device that converts electrical signals into audible sound.

1.2.9 Hardware Interfaces

A hardware interface (port) is a point of connection where external devices are physically connected to the computer's system unit so that data can be transferred between them.

Parallel Port
Transfers multiple bits of data simultaneously over several wires; was commonly used to connect older printers.
Serial Port
Transfers data one bit at a time over a single wire; slower than parallel but works well over long distances; used for older mouse/modem connections.
USB (Universal Serial Bus)
A modern, high-speed and widely-used port for connecting almost any external device such as pen drives, keyboards, mouse, and printers.
HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface)
Transfers high-definition video and audio signals together through a single cable; commonly used to connect a computer to a monitor, projector or television.
Expansion Slots
Slots on the motherboard where additional cards (such as a graphics card, sound card or network card) can be inserted to add extra features to the computer.

Chapter Summary

A computer is an electronic device that accepts input, processes it and gives output, and it is known for its speed, accuracy, storage capacity and versatility. Computers evolved through five generations, moving from vacuum tubes to artificial intelligence based systems. Computers are broadly classified into super, mainframe, mini and microcomputers based on size, speed and cost, and mobile computing has made computing possible almost anywhere. A computer system is made up of input, processing (CPU), memory and output units connected together through buses, and works with the help of various input devices, output devices, memory types (RAM, ROM, cache, secondary storage) and hardware interfaces such as USB and HDMI.

Key Terms

TermMeaning
CPUCentral Processing Unit - the "brain" of the computer
ALUArithmetic Logic Unit - performs calculations and comparisons
RAMVolatile primary memory used for temporary storage
ROMNon-volatile memory storing permanent instructions like BIOS
BusPathway used to transfer data/address/control signals
MicroprocessorSingle chip containing the complete CPU

Important Points to Remember

  • Computer works on the principle: Input → Process → Output.
  • 1 Byte = 8 Bits, and each higher storage unit is 1024 times the lower one.
  • RAM is volatile; ROM is non-volatile.
  • Cache memory sits between CPU and main memory to speed up processing.
  • MICR is mainly used by banks; OMR is mainly used for checking exam sheets.
