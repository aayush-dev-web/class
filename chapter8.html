CHAPTER 8

INFORMATION SECURITY AND CYBER LAW

8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is one in which computers, the internet and digital devices are deeply integrated into daily life - communication, education, business and governance.

Computer Ethics refers to the moral principles that guide the appropriate and responsible use of computers and the internet.

  • Do not use a computer to harm other people
  • Do not access, copy, or use another person's data without permission
  • Respect others' privacy online
  • Do not use computers to spread false information
  • Respect copyright and intellectual property

8.2 Information Security

Information security refers to the practice of protecting data and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification or destruction.

PrincipleMeaning
ConfidentialityEnsuring that information is accessible only to authorized people
IntegrityEnsuring that data is accurate and not altered without authorization
AvailabilityEnsuring that information and systems are accessible when needed

8.3 Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities carried out using computers or the internet.

TypeDescription
HackingGaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network
PhishingTricking users into revealing personal information through fake emails or websites
Identity TheftStealing someone's personal information to commit fraud
CyberbullyingUsing digital platforms to harass, threaten or embarrass another person
Online FraudDeceiving people for financial gain through fake offers, online scams, or fake shopping sites

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

TermDescription
VirusA malicious program that attaches itself to files and spreads by replicating itself, damaging data
WormA self-replicating malicious program that spreads across networks without needing to attach to a file
Trojan HorseMalicious software disguised as legitimate software that creates a backdoor for attackers
SpywareSoftware that secretly monitors and collects user information without consent
RansomwareMalware that locks or encrypts a user's data and demands payment to restore access
SpamUnwanted, irrelevant bulk messages (usually emails) sent to a large number of users, often for advertising or scams

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

  • Use strong, unique passwords and change them regularly
  • Install and update reliable antivirus and anti-malware software
  • Do not click on suspicious links or open unknown email attachments
  • Use two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever possible
  • Keep operating systems and software updated with the latest security patches
  • Avoid sharing personal or financial information on unverified websites
  • Regularly back up important data

8.6 Intellectual Property Right (IPR)

Intellectual Property Right is the legal right that gives the creator of an original work (invention, literary work, design, software) exclusive control over its use and distribution.

TypeProtects
CopyrightOriginal literary, artistic and software works
PatentNew inventions and technical processes
TrademarkBrand names, logos and symbols

8.7 Digital Signature

A digital signature is an electronic, encrypted form of authentication used to verify the identity of the sender of a digital message or document, and to ensure that the content has not been altered.

It works using public key cryptography: the sender signs the document with a private key, and the receiver verifies it using the sender's public key.

8.8 Cyber Law in Nepal

Nepal has enacted the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063 B.S. (2008 A.D.), commonly known as the Cyber Law of Nepal, to provide legal recognition to electronic transactions, digital signatures, and to control cybercrime activities in the country.

Main objectives of the Electronic Transaction Act:

  • To provide legal recognition to electronic records and digital signatures
  • To control and punish computer-related crimes such as hacking and data theft
  • To promote the use of e-commerce and e-governance in Nepal
  • To protect intellectual property rights in the digital environment

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

The ICT Policy of Nepal is a government policy framework designed to promote the development and use of Information and Communication Technology in the country.

Key objectives of the ICT Policy:

  • To develop ICT infrastructure across the country, including rural areas
  • To promote e-governance for efficient and transparent public service delivery
  • To develop skilled human resources in the ICT sector
  • To promote the domestic IT industry and attract investment
  • To ensure equal access to ICT services for all citizens
